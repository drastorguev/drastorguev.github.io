Insurance Asset-Based Finance Data Operations: Governance, Controls and Reporting

Project statement

An investment manager running credit portfolios for insurance clients needs its asset-based finance (ABF) data to be accurate, timely and decision-ready. Deal, collateral and servicer data arrives from many originators, servicers and trustees in inconsistent formats, and feeds valuations, surveillance, risk and statutory reporting. The project establishes governance, controls and daily data operations for that flow, led from Bangalore and working alongside New York and London teams.

Requirements

  • Reliable intake and normalisation of loan tapes, trustee reports and servicer data into standard internal models.
  • Daily and weekly controls over funding and advance activity, unfunded commitments and collateral eligibility exceptions.
  • Reconciliation of facility balances, cash, interest and collateral positions against servicers, trustees, agents and administrators, with every break driven to resolution.
  • Data that supports insurer statutory and regulatory reporting, including NAIC designations and SSAP 26R and 43R data elements, and is audit-ready.
  • Documented handoffs and overlapping hours, so issues move between regions without being lost.
  • Data-usage and PII controls agreed with compliance and legal.

Approach

I would start from the controls and build outwards.

  1. Controls before convenience: Every dataset passes pre- and post-ingestion checks for completeness, duplicates, referential integrity and anomalies before anyone relies on it.
  2. One canonical deal model: Each originator and servicer has a documented mapping into the standard model. New deals then onboard by configuring a mapping, not by rebuilding a pipeline.
  3. Follow-the-sun coverage: A documented handoff at the open and close of each regional day, with incident playbooks and coverage rotations, so no item depends on one person’s memory.

Trade-off: Standardisation against speed. Unusual structures, such as bespoke warehouse or forward-flow facilities, take longer to onboard. I would accept a slower first-time setup for those in exchange for a model every downstream team can trust, and document each exception.

Implementation

  • Ingestion and normalisation: Snowflake pipelines built with snowflake-snowpark-python or the Snowflake connector, with dbt holding the mapping templates for loan tapes, borrowing base data, remittance detail and trustee reports. Mappings are kept as version-controlled configuration, so new deals onboard without a code change.
  • Control framework: Great Expectations or Pandera checks for completeness, duplicates, referential integrity and anomalies, run before and after each load. Failures are logged to a named owner, with Airflow or Dagster sending alerts when a check fails.
  • Reconciliation: pandas merges with tolerance thresholds match facility balances, cash, interest and collateral positions to servicer, trustee and administrator statements. rapidfuzz handles variations in counterparty and deal names, and aged breaks are reported daily on a dashboard.
  • Pricing and analytics feeds: Python connectors for Bloomberg through blpapi, ICE and rating agency data through vendor APIs or file drops, and Intex outputs aligned to valuation and risk models. Data contracts track vendor SLAs and change notices.
  • Reporting: Surveillance dashboards in Power BI or Plotly Dash covering delinquency, default, loss, recovery, prepayment, credit enhancement and overcollateralisation. A statutory data pack for NAIC and SSAP reporting is generated by a Python pipeline with openpyxl, from the same governed tables.
  • Lineage and governance: Alation catalogue and lineage, with OpenLineage events emitted from Airflow or dbt where supported, documented SOPs, data-access controls and change control on mappings through Git.

Indicative Roadmap

Phase Timing Deliverable
Current state and inventory Weeks 1–3 Inventory of deals, feeds, controls and reconciliations, with gaps and owners identified
Controls and standards Weeks 4–6 Control framework, SOPs, canonical model and mapping templates
Ingestion and reconciliation automation Weeks 6–12 Automated intake, checks and daily reconciliation for priority deals
Reporting and statutory data Weeks 10–14 Surveillance dashboards, statutory data pack and audit trail
Handover and run model Weeks 14–16 Coverage rotations live, playbooks tested and a post-implementation review completed

Measures of success

  • Daily controls and reconciliations are completed by the agreed cut-off, every day.
  • Aged reconciliation breaks are reduced, and each one has an owner and a date.
  • Vendor and servicer files arrive in the expected format and on schedule, with changes notified in advance.
  • Statutory and audit data can be traced back to source through lineage.
  • Each regional handoff is documented, and no open item is left unowned overnight.
